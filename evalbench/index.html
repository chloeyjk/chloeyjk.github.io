EvalBench

Compare two agents run on the same tasks. Paste per-task success counts to get 95% confidence intervals, a paired difference, pass@k, pass^k, and how many tasks you'd need to detect the observed difference. Everything runs in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

What the numbers mean

Success rate ± 95% CI. Each task's rate is successes ÷ trials. The interval is mean ± 1.96 × SE, with SE computed across tasks, so it treats the tasks as a sample from a larger population of tasks (Miller 2024).

Paired difference. Uses per-task differences A − B, which cancels shared task difficulty and usually gives a much tighter interval than comparing two separate means. If the interval excludes 0, the difference is significant at the 5% level.

pass@k / pass^k. The chance at least one of k tries succeeds (Chen et al. 2021) versus the chance all k succeed (Yao et al. 2024), estimated without bias from each task's n trials and averaged over tasks.

Tasks for 80% power. n = ((z(0.975) + z(0.8)) × SD of differences ÷ difference)², the paired sample size needed to detect a difference as large as the one observed.

Caveats. This uses a normal approximation, so treat it as rough when there are fewer than about 30 tasks. It also assumes tasks are independent; if tasks share a source (for example, several tasks from one repository), you need clustered standard errors.
