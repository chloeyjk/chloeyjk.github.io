Weekly scan

Finds candidates for the catalog. The agent returns a list and does not edit files.

Find work from the past 7 days on evaluating AI agents or LLMs where the
statistical method is a real contribution: uncertainty quantification,
paired comparison, judge reliability, benchmark validity, or repeated-trial
reliability.

For each candidate, open its source page and report:
- exact title, first author, date, and URL (arXiv abs / ACL Anthology / publisher)
- one sentence: what statistical method it uses or proposes
- one sentence: why it matters for agent evaluation
- which topic fits: uncertainty | paired | judges | validity | multi-trial | practice

Drop anything whose source page you did not open. Do not guess citations.
Return at most 10, strongest first.

Paper reading card

Fill this in before writing a catalog summary.

Paper:
Estimand: what quantity is being estimated, over what population of tasks?
Design: tasks × agents × trials? Paired? Clustered? Judge-scored?
Uncertainty: how is it reported (SE, CI, bootstrap, none)?
Assumptions: independence, exchangeability, judge accuracy, ...
What would change my mind: the check or data that would undercut the result
One-line summary for the catalog:

Eval report checklist

For writing up any agent eval of my own.

[ ] Number of tasks, trials per task, and how tasks were sampled
[ ] Score with 95% CI (clustered if tasks share a source)
[ ] Comparisons are paired over the same tasks, with CI of the difference
[ ] pass@k / pass^k reported with k and trials per task stated
[ ] Judge-scored metrics: judge model, prompt, and agreement with a human audit set
[ ] Cost (tokens or dollars) next to accuracy
[ ] Power: smallest difference this design can detect
[ ] Raw per-trial results released or archived
