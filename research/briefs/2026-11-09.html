New this week

Reliability

Few Trials, Many Metrics: Beta Mixtures for Low-Budget AI Evaluation

Method: Fits a BIC-selected mixture of Beta distributions to per-instance success rates from only a handful of trials, then derives Pass@k, Pass^k and AUC@k, with delta-method confidence intervals, in closed form with no extra model calls.

Why it matters: Repeated-sampling metrics are expensive to estimate well; this extrapolates accurately beyond the observed number of trials on math and science benchmarks, and the intervals cover the full-data reference.

Tironi et al. · arXiv 2026 [Source]
LLM Judges

VoirDire: Counterfactual Calibration for Reliable LLM-as-a-Judge Aggregation

Method: Makes controlled counterfactual edits to a response's surface attributes (length, formatting, fluency), measures how much each judge's score shifts, and down-weights those bias-sensitive directions when aggregating a judge panel.

Why it matters: Judge panels can agree because they share the same surface biases, not because the output is actually better; across 12 datasets this cuts error by up to 26.8% on continuous scoring and improves accuracy by up to 5.3 points on binary/preference tasks.

Shin & Sala · arXiv 2026 [Source]
LLM Judges

Quantiles, Not Points: Streaming Quantile Matching for Ordinal-Aware LLM-as-a-Judge Calibration

Method: Treats LLM-judge scores as draws from a distribution rather than isolated points and calibrates them to a small human-labeled anchor set with streaming quantile matching, an optimal-transport map that preserves the anchor set's ordinal structure.

Why it matters: Beats prior post-hoc calibrators on distributional fidelity and narrows the accuracy gap between smaller and larger judge models, which could make cheaper judges more viable.

Subasic et al. · arXiv 2026 [Source]
LLM Judges

Multi-Estimate Elicitation Does Not Uniformly Debias Language Model Judgments

Method: A preregistered study of 45 confirmatory tests across five classic judgment-bias tasks (anchoring, hindsight, base-rate, conjunction, status-quo) and six model configurations, comparing single-estimate against multi-estimate elicitation.

Why it matters: A popular debiasing trick — asking the model for several weighted estimates instead of one — does not uniformly reduce these biases, and raises post-outcome confirmation error by about 10.7 points in at least one configuration.

Jhang · arXiv 2026 [Source]
Validity

Graph-Based Testing of Conditional Mean Independence with Applications to Large Language Model Evaluation

Method: A graph-based conditional-mean-independence test extending the edge-count principle, with sample-splitting or cross-fitting giving analytic p-values under an asymptotic normal null.

Why it matters: Applied to LLM response preferences, it gives a principled way to check whether an LLM judge's verdicts are explained by response length or other recorded covariates rather than genuine quality.

Liu et al. · arXiv 2026 [Source]
LLM Judges

Calibration-Constrained Diagnostics for Evaluating LLM Self-Evaluation

Method: Category-level calibration diagnostics (PVC and C-PVC), aggregated with a Volume Under Surface and a positive-margin restriction, applied across 13 models and four reasoning benchmarks.

Why it matters: A model that looks strongest under standard aggregate calibration metrics can still fail this category-level check, exposing act-or-defer failures that ECE and Brier score miss.

Woo et al. · arXiv 2026 [Source]
Reliability

One Run Is Not Enough: A Monte Carlo Protocol for Evaluating LLM Reliability on Data Science Tasks

Method: DS-QA, 100 data-science and statistics questions from UK university courses across 16 datasets and three difficulty levels, each run 100 times per model under a Monte Carlo protocol instead of a single pass.

Why it matters: Finds substantial run-to-run variability on data-visualization and supervised-learning tasks that a one-shot accuracy score would hide, arguing consistency should be reported alongside quality.

Ni et al. · arXiv 2026 [Source]
In Practice

Independent Assessment of AI R&D Acceleration at Anthropic in October 2026

Method: A breakpoint OLS trend fit on a benchmark capability index, with stratified-bootstrap confidence intervals and a sensitivity analysis across six link-function choices for the index.

Why it matters: A rare case of a lab publishing its uncertainty analysis for a headline capability claim: the main estimate is 1.9x acceleration (95% CI 1.5-2.8x), but the point estimate ranges from 1.1x to 2.9x depending on the link function alone.
