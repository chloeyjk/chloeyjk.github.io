Weekly brief · 2026-09-29

Budget-constrained uncertainty, anytime-valid leaderboards, and a reality check on run-to-run noise

Seven new papers this week, several sharing a theme: how much of a reported benchmark gap is signal versus sampling, protocol, or evaluation-run noise, and how to spend a fixed evaluation budget honestly.

New this week

Reliability

Speculative Evaluation of Stochastic LLMs

Method: Hierarchical Bayesian model of task-level success with Neyman allocation, used to route a fixed rollout budget toward the tasks with the most outcome variance.

Why it matters: Cuts the variance of benchmark score estimates by roughly 13-34% versus sampling every task equally, at no extra rollout cost.

Shen et al. · arXiv 2026 [Source]
Uncertainty

Rank Confidence Sequences:Anytime-valid Leaderboards

Method: Confidence sequences for each model's leaderboard rank, built from betting e-processes combined with closed testing across possible orderings.

Why it matters: A leaderboard can be inspected after every new score without inflating the error rate, and individual models can stop evaluation early once their rank is settled.

Khosravi & Huo · arXiv 2026 [Source]
Uncertainty

Sharp Limits for Honest Uncertainty in Hard-Budget Repeated Evaluation

Method: Minimax-optimal confidence-interval width for repeated evaluation under a fixed response budget, paired with a task-covering sampling design and a joint mean/disagreement interval.

Why it matters: On a 16-model LiveCodeBench replay, the design cuts median estimation error by 87% and interval width by 31% versus pooled uniform sampling at the same budget.

Cheng et al. · arXiv 2026 [Source]
Comparing Agents

How Sensitive Are LLM Leaderboard Claims to Hidden Model Selection?

Method: A Gaussian selection model gives a sensitivity curve for how many unreported hidden model variants would erase a claimed leaderboard win.

Why it matters: Auditing 394 comparisons on the Open LLM Leaderboard, 391 lack statistical support even before accounting for hidden selection.

Yang & Chen · arXiv 2026 [Source]
Reliability

Identical Runs, Different Results: Benchmarking AI Coding Agents on Open-Weight Models

Method: A repeated-measures design reruns six coding-agent/model pairings 52 times each (584 runs total) under fixed conditions.

Why it matters: Run-to-run variance within a single pairing exceeds the differences between pairings, so tens to over a hundred repeated runs are needed before an agent comparison is reliable.

Ariño de la Rubia & Pafka · arXiv 2026 [Source]
Validity

Artificial Societies Benchmark: A Validation Framework for Synthetic Research

Method: Eleven tests of internal, construct, and external validity, applied to nine LLMs used as synthetic survey respondents against twenty human data sources.

Why it matters: Models can match average human responses while still compressing response ranges and distorting the relationships between traits.

Chidichimo et al. · arXiv 2026 [Source]
Validity

A Function-Level Vulnerability Score Measures Flag Rate More Than the Model: Protocol Effects on Paired Benchmarks

Method: Holds model outputs fixed and varies only the scoring protocol (metric choice, verdict extraction, output budget) on a paired vulnerability-detection benchmark, then correlates protocol choices with the reported score.

Why it matters: Function-level F1 tracks how often a model flags both functions in a pair (Spearman +0.86) far more than it tracks actual pair-level correctness (+0.16), a warning about what paired benchmark scores are really measuring.

Cichoń & Dmitruk · arXiv 2026 [Source]
