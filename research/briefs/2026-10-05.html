Weekly brief · 2026-10-05

Decomposing leaderboard variance, and when LLM judges rank right but measure wrong

Four new papers this week, two of them digging into the same crack: an LLM judge (or an agent leaderboard) can get the relative order right while still being badly wrong about the underlying scale.

← All briefs · Covers 2026-09-29 to 2026-10-05

New this week

Uncertainty

Agent Evaluation Reliability: More Tasks Won't (Always) Fix An Agent Leaderboard

Method: A Bayesian variance-decomposition model applied to 22 agent benchmarks, splitting leaderboard rank variation into contributions from the underlying model, the scaffold, and task design.

Why it matters: Reliability for a fixed model-scaffold pairing is high (0.94-0.99), but reliability of the underlying model alone is often much lower (0.15-0.84); pooling diverse benchmarks helps more than just adding tasks, raising reliability from 0.44 to 0.75 at up to 83% lower cost.

Hardy et al. · arXiv 2026 [Source]
LLM Judges

Right Order, Wrong Scale: Auditing LLM Judges for Occupational AI Measurement

Method: Cross-validated calibration and prediction-assisted estimation, applied to 33 LLM-judge configurations audited against 45,796 human worker ratings.

Why it matters: Judges largely agree on how to rank responses, but their estimated acceptance rates range from 3% to 98% against a 61% human consensus — rank agreement does not guarantee valid aggregate measurement.

Lyu & Thompson · arXiv 2026 [Source]
LLM Judges

Pair Difficulty Matters: Rethinking Pairwise LLM-as-a-Judge Evaluation and Consistency

Method: Bradley-Terry pairwise-aggregation geometry, controlled simulation, and two human-rated corpora, used to show standard judge proxies are dominated by hard-to-call near-tied pairs.

Why it matters: Position bias, transitivity, and pairwise agreement correlate only weakly with ranking accuracy against gold; the paper proposes rank-gap-conditional metrics that weight the pairs that actually carry signal.

Brocai & Becker · arXiv 2026 [Source]
Validity

QC-Stark: A Multi-Task Benchmark Revealing Capability Dissociations in LLMs Evaluated on Quantum Computing Tasks

Method: A 2-parameter item response theory model fit to 10 LLMs across 11 execution-graded quantum-computing tasks, used to check the benchmark's own measurement quality.

Why it matters: Overall rankings mask substantial per-task variation: on 4 of 11 tasks, task-specific rank correlates with overall rank at a level not statistically distinguishable from chance.

Gupta · arXiv 2026 [Source]

Considered, not added

  • Optimal Design for Active Preference Learning with Biased LLM Judges (arXiv 2609.38860): a judge-bias-aware acquisition method for active preference learning — the contribution is to alignment training, not to evaluation methodology.
  • Prediction-powered Neural Architecture Search (arXiv 2610.01317): applies prediction-powered inference, but to neural architecture search rather than evaluating LLMs or agents.
  • Mathematical Transfer in LLMs Follows Reasoning Approach More Than Topic (arXiv 2610.00331): rigorous counterbalanced design and confidence intervals, but it's a transfer-learning study, not an evaluation-methods contribution.
  • Measuring Collapse and Correction in Homogeneous-Panel LLM Debate (arXiv 2609.35279): uses correlation tests and p-values as supporting analysis of multi-agent debate dynamics, but statistics aren't the paper's evaluation-methods contribution.
  • Open-Endedness Bench: Measuring Epistemic Process from Agent Records (arXiv 2610.02588): a graph-based, qualitative verification approach for agent research logs — no statistical method.
