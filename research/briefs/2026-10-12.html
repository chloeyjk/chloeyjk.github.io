New this week

Uncertainty

Certified Corruption Budgets: Anytime-Valid Leaderboard Claims under Adaptive Rigging

Method: Betting-style e-processes publish, after every new vote, a certified bound on how many corrupted or rigged records a ranking claim can tolerate, valid at all times at once under continuous monitoring.

Why it matters: On a replay of 1.8 million Chatbot Arena votes, a few hundred rigged votes are enough to fool standard confidence intervals into certifying a false ordering, while the certified bound still holds.

Validity

On the estimation and validity of AI time horizons---a statistical look at the METR plot

Method: Refits the 50%-time-horizon curve with splines and item response theory instead of assuming AI difficulty is linear in log human-completion-time, and checks construct validity with cross-validated proper scoring rules.

Why it matters: The fitted difficulty curve is nearly flat for short tasks and steep for long ones, so a jump from 3 to 30 minutes is much easier for an AI than 30 minutes to 5 hours, even though both are 10x increases.

Nguyen & Fithian · arXiv 2026 [Source]
LLM Judges

All Verdicts are Not Equal: Rethinking LLM Judge Reliability

Method: Ten repeats per condition across six judge models, four benchmarks, five prompt formats, two presentation orders, and three sampling temperatures, feeding a "trustworthy verdict rate" requiring reproducible, order-invariant, and accurate judgments.

Why it matters: The metric yields a theoretical bound on how much position bias alone can limit judge accuracy, and compares pairwise win-rate evaluation with holistic rubric scoring.

Kumar et al. · arXiv 2026 [Source]
Validity

Searching for "Harmful Refusal": A Psychometric Audit of an AI Safety Benchmark

Method: Multidimensional item response theory plus differential item functioning analysis, applied to HarmBench after finding three of four candidate HELM Safety datasets are saturated.

Why it matters: "Harmful refusal" does not behave as one coherent attribute, and cross-developer scoring gaps mostly disappear once comparisons are matched on item scope, warning against treating aggregate safety scores as a single valid measurement.

Stewart et al. · arXiv 2026 [Source]
Reliability

What pass@k Cannot Measure: Evaluating Diversity and Capability Retention after Post-Training

Method: Controlled comparisons of GRPO versus rejection-sampling fine-tuning, with token-level entropy, answer-level entropy, and unique-answers-per-prompt as diversity metrics alongside pass@k.

Why it matters: pass@k depends only on each problem's marginal success probability and shows no consistent capability winner even when the two training methods move diversity in opposite directions, so it can certify a property it does not measure.

Rath et al. · arXiv 2026 [Source]
