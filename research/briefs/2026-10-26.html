New this week

Comparing Agents

Beyond Scalar Leaderboards: Adaptive Sampling for Pareto Frontier Identification in Multi-Objective LLM Arenas

Method: A fixed-confidence adaptive-sampling algorithm (VB-EGE) that runs pairwise arena comparisons under a multi-objective Bradley-Terry model, eliminating models once enough statistical evidence certifies their Pareto status, with a near-minimax-optimal sample-complexity bound.

Why it matters: Arena-style human evaluation is expensive; this gives a principled stopping rule for identifying which models are actually on the frontier without exhausting the comparison budget, validated on three real arena datasets.

Uncertainty

Reliable AI-Assisted Decisions via Selective Auditing

Method: Online constrained optimization with distribution-free, provable error-control guarantees on the fraction of unaudited AI errors, including an asymptotically optimal variant under both adversarial and i.i.d. streams.

Why it matters: A rigorous way to decide when an AI decision (with a confidence or verifier score) needs human review, tested directly on screening harmful LLM requests.

Gibbs et al. · arXiv 2026 [Source]
Uncertainty

Lifetime-valid decisions by continually evolving AI analysts

Method: Continual validation: each proposed update of an evolving AI system is tested against simulated null-hypothesis data before deployment, with a proof that the lifetime probability of ever deploying an over-the-limit version stays bounded.

Why it matters: Addresses a real production failure mode — Type I error creeping up as an AI system is repeatedly updated — cutting observed error from 0.098-0.183 down to 0.044-0.045 in bioequivalence and A/B-test examples while keeping most of the power.

Sun et al. · arXiv 2026 [Source]
Validity

SkillEval: Learning Interpretable Ability Profiles of LLMs via Cognitive Diagnosis Models

Method: Cognitive diagnosis models from psychometrics, applied to automatically-discovered per-question skills, to estimate a per-skill ability profile for each model instead of one aggregate score.

Why it matters: Across 3,811 models and 9,523 items on five benchmarks, the skill profiles predict held-out item behavior better than average scores, and support cheaper routing and adaptive testing.

Arda et al. · arXiv 2026 [Source]
LLM Judges

Behind Aggregate Scores: What Systematically Varying Arena-Hard's LLM Judge Reveals

Method: A full factorial design over eight LLM-judge instruction variants (criteria, output format, task framing), each repeated unchanged at temperature zero, comparing aggregate-score shifts against prompt-level outcome changes.

Why it matters: An unchanged rerun of the same judge setup alone flips the win/tie/loss outcome on 21.2% of prompts even though the aggregate score barely moves — a caution against trusting a stable aggregate as evidence that individual verdicts are repeatable.

Yorke · arXiv 2026 [Source]

Considered, not added

  • Prompting Strategy Effects Depend on the Judge: A Multilingual Study of LLM-as-a-Judge Robustness (arXiv 2610.31172): a large, careful empirical study (9 prompting strategies × 4 judges × 27 languages), but no new statistical method — kept out to keep the bar on methodological contribution.
  • Benchmarking the Quantum Leap: Auditing LLM Evaluation for Quantum Code Generation (arXiv 2610.28678): reports bootstrap confidence intervals and multiplicity-adjusted comparisons, but these are standard reporting practice applied to a new benchmark, not a methodological contribution.
  • Beyond Binary Labels: Diagnostic Evaluation of LLM Safety with Prompt-Level Rubrics (arXiv 2610.29225): a rubric-based safety benchmark; its Cohen's κ and agreement numbers validate the judging setup rather than contribute a method.
  • SANE: Automated Scorer-Specific Metamorphic Testing for LLM Judges (arXiv 2610.28886): a metamorphic-testing framework for judges; Spearman correlation is used only to validate against human labels, not as the paper's core method.
  • Elicitation or Expansion? Demystifying Gains from Agent Harness Optimization (arXiv 2610.27889): uses pass@k as an evaluation lens on harness optimization, but introduces no new inferential method.
  • Alternative Strategies for Active Learning with Bayesian Bradley-Terry Model (arXiv 2610.29416): a Bayesian Bradley-Terry active-learning method with real statistical content, but all experiments are synthetic and the paper is not framed around LLM/agent evaluation.
