4 new entries this week, spanning a pre-registered fairness-evaluation protocol, a data-reuse audit for benchmark leakage, and two judge-reliability findings — one on grading proxy-optimization gains, one on writer-model self-preference bias.

New this week

Validity

Qistas: A Pre-Registered, Portable Protocol for Fairness Evaluation of Large Language Models

Method: Every comparison is paired by construction and analyzed with sign-flip permutation tests, exact McNemar tests, template-clustered bootstrap intervals, and Holm-corrected multiple comparisons, backed by a power analysis fixed before data collection that accounts for the design effect of template clustering.

Why it matters: A protocol paper (no model results yet) built around a real failure mode: fairness benchmarks translated across societies carry categories that don't apply, and its pilot audit found 24 of 25 template groups in a 58-item seed bank failed at least one check before any model was even run.

Validity

ReuScope: Auditing Data Reuse and Evaluation Sensitivity in Capsule Vision 2024

Method: Paired-seed removal experiments, matched controls, a difference-in-differences estimate, and paired-t confidence intervals, applied across ten paired seeds to a benchmark with byte-identical files crossing the train/validation boundary.

Why it matters: Removing the overlapping source data drops accuracy on the public validation split (82.5% to 61.2%) but improves it on a different split — the paper's point is that this kind of swing measures sensitivity to source removal, not leakage-inflated scores, and the two are easy to conflate.

LLM Judges

Predicting Human-Gain Curves under Local Proxy Optimization from Repeated Ratings

Method: Models optimizing against a fixed proxy (e.g., an LLM-judge score) as a KL-regularized exponential tilt, estimating a first-order alignment coefficient and a second-order correction from repeated human ratings on calibration items to predict the human-gain curve on held-out items.

Why it matters: Average proxy-human correlation alone can't tell you whether pushing harder on a proxy (including an LLM-judge proxy) keeps helping or starts hurting real quality; this gives a pre-optimization diagnostic for that, tested on summarization, translation, and a GPT-4o judge-proxy experiment.

Choi, Nam & Lee · arXiv 2026 [Source]
LLM Judges

AI evaluators favor job applications written by certain AI models, independent of candidate quality

Method: A multi-model auditing framework crossing 11 LLM writer agents with 9 evaluator agents over 10 jobs and 50 candidates each, identifying a consistent global writer-preference ranking and self-preference bias, then using feature analysis to trace both to shared stylistic cues rather than an unmeasured per-writer "fingerprint."

Why it matters: In an all-AI hiring pipeline, which model a candidate used to write their cover letter measurably changes their odds — about 1.8 of 25 qualified candidates filtered out purely on model mismatch in the worst pairing — independent of how good the application actually is.

Pagan et al. · arXiv 2026 [Source]

Considered, not added

  • Towards Understanding Score-Seeking in LLMs (arXiv 2610.41014): a careful behavioral study of reward-hacking/score-seeking in production models, but no statistical evaluation method is the contribution — it's about why models game graders, not how to measure evaluations.
  • Counterfactual Pairwise Augmentation Improves Small Pointwise LLM Judges (arXiv 2610.41149): improves small judges via joint pointwise/counterfactual-pairwise training; its evaluation is macro-F1 against a frontier judge, not a new statistical method.
  • Multi-Test Nation Bias Benchmarking of LLMs with Explicit Unbiased Baselines (arXiv 2610.41486): constructs pseudo-unbiased baselines from UN voting records for a bias benchmark, but doesn't foreground an inferential statistical method beyond baseline comparison.
  • Real Computation or Merely Naming? A Pre-Registered Mechanism Audit of 25 Cognitive-Architecture Modules (arXiv 2610.41604): a pre-registered audit, but the method is a structured code/trace audit with pass/fail counts, not a statistical technique.
  • One Verdict Is Not Enough: Regression Testing Learned World Models and Their Test Harnesses (arXiv 2610.41130): a reliability protocol for video world-model test harnesses (outcome-class counts, verdict rates), close to this catalog's scope but about physical world models rather than LLMs/agents.
  • ANGLER / REEF: Verifier-Grounded Benchmarking with an Adaptive Attacker for Web Agents (arXiv 2610.41272): a solid new security benchmark for web agents, but no statistical evaluation method beyond state-based verification.
