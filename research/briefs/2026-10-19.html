LLM Judges

A CaLLM Judge is a Better Judge: Mitigating Bias in LLM-as-a-judge

Method: Treats each known LLM-judge bias (position, verbosity, model family) as miscalibration on an identifiable group, and applies multicalibration to bound bias across many groups simultaneously, including ones not specified in advance.

Why it matters: Proves conditions under which this improves the judge's accuracy rather than trading it off against calibration, and shows reduced multicalibration error and better accuracy on pairwise preference benchmarks.

Uncertainty

Limits of Statistical Assurance for Embodied and Agentic AI under Adaptive Adversaries

Method: Derives a closed-form bound on expected post-certification safety violations from an adversary that preserves unattacked behavior until a statistical safety test certifies it, covering adaptive and privately randomized evaluators.

Why it matters: Validated in simulated robot-collaboration and preregistered randomized AgentDojo trials; a mixture likelihood-ratio monitor with revocation keeps cumulative excess violations bounded, cutting them from 12.00 to 3.81 per 24 assigned task opportunities.

Singh · arXiv 2026 [Source]
Validity

Evaluating Model Capabilities After Benchmark Saturation

Method: Simulates benchmark saturation retrospectively using historical benchmark and model releases, then tests whether protocols applied only to saturated benchmarks' solved transcripts can predict capability on held-out, unsaturated items.

Why it matters: Pairwise LLM judging of transcripts cuts prediction error 5.3x versus a baseline using only saturated scores, though the signal does not always generalize across distribution shifts, such as underestimating early-2025 reasoning models.

Hamada et al. · arXiv 2026 [Source]
Validity

ProbabilityBench: Model Evaluation and Calibration with Exact Ground-Truth Uncertainty

Method: Builds simulated text-based domains with analytically exact conditional class probabilities, so reported model probabilities can be scored directly against ground truth via KL divergence instead of the usual single-observed-label proxy.

Why it matters: Also serves as a meta-evaluation framework, using the known ground truth to benchmark the accuracy of standard calibration metrics and Bayes-error estimators themselves, not just the 16 models tested.

Dai et al. · arXiv 2026 [Source]
LLM Judges

Fantastic Metrics and How to Find Them: Meta-Evaluating Metrics for Multi-lingual Style-Personalized Generation

Method: Argues metric-human correlation is an unstable, inappropriate estimate of a metric's evaluation capability, and proposes a regression-based meta-evaluation that learns on calibrated metric outputs to predict human ratings, scored against a constant-mean baseline and human agreement.

Why it matters: Across 40 evaluation settings spanning 10 languages, simple string- and embedding-based metrics can match strong LLM judges once calibrated, reframing how metric quality should be measured at all.

Jangra & de Wynter · arXiv 2026 [Source]
LLM Judges

Cheap, Consistent, Calibrated, but Biased? Typed Decision Models as Judges

Method: Runs the CALM benchmark's twelve bias tests on eight typed decision models (which output a probability per answer instead of generated text) alongside seven LLM judges from 2024 and 2026.

Why it matters: Every typed decision model is biased on three to eight of twelve tests, and good calibration does not protect against bias, even though these models are 37x cheaper and more consistent under answer-order swaps than current LLM judges.

Lukassen et al. · arXiv 2026 [Source]
